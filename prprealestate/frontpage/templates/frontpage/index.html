My Favorite Seattle Neighborhoods:

Having been raised in Seattle since the early '90s, I've had a front row seat to this city's transformation into the tech giant that it is today. Currently, Seattle is undergoing an extensive change spurred by the success of Amazon, which has now surpassed Microsoft as the richest publicly owned company in the world.

As a result, Seattle is assuming its place among major vibrant, energetic metropolises like New York City and Los Angeles. With this growth, the city offers increased amentities and highlights its best offerings for anyone local or just visiting.

Ballard: Ballard Market

Biscuit & Bean, Reuben's Brews, Gracia, Stone Gardens, Ballard Locks, Trailbend Taprooms, and Ballard Market are just a few names that I think about when someone says, "Let's go to Ballard". I can spend an entire day roaming around Ballard and still not see all that Ballard has to offer. Ever been to the Ballard Locks during the summer and watch Salmon pass through to travel 40-50 miles upriver to spawn? Sockeye, with over 50,000 per year, can be seen from mid-June to mid-July. Chinook average around 10,000 per year with peak viewing mostly in August. Coho average around 15,000 per year with peak viewing in September. Steelhead, unfortunately, are no longer seen at the Locks.

Fremont: Fremont Market

Arguably one of my favorite Seattle neighborhoods and if I could live anywhere in Seattle, this would be it! Fremont Troll, Lenin Statue, Fremont Bridge, Rocket, Guidepost, Waiting for the Interurban, Dinosaur Topiary, Ship Canal, and Sri Chimnoy are all a must see if you're ever in the neighborhood. Of course, one of my favorite sites to see whenever I am in the neighborhood is the Fremont Troll. But as much as I love the Fremont Troll, there is so much more to Fremont: Fremont Brewing Company, Schilling Cider House, Fainting Goat Gelato, Theo Chocolate, Jive Time Records, Portage Bay Goods, and Milstead & Co. I love Fremont even more because you're a hop, skip, and a jump from Gas Works Park which is absolutely amazing during the summer and if you love running and/or biking, the Burke-Gilman Trail is right around the corner.

Queen Anne: Queen Anne Market

If there is one neighborhood in the Seattle that has changed more than any other, it's probably going to be Queen Anne. I know this because I went to middle school there: New Options Middle School, also knows as just N.O.M.S. This was back in the mid to late '90s and I'll never forget it. The school is no longer there but the building remains. You can read more about it here; it was actually built around 1862. I love Queen Anne because it sits on top of a hill and is super close to the Space Needle, Chihuly Garden and Glass, and Seattle Center. The reason this is important is because once a year during about the third week of the month of July, this happens, The Bite of Seattle and it's at the Seattle Center. It is arguably one of my favorite events of the year and I make every effort to go. I love the entertainment, the food, the beer, and all the attractions this event brings. If you've never been, I highly recommend it. In addition to all of this, if you're ever in the neighborhood and you're craving something delicious, I highly recommend Bounty Kitchen or Toulouse Petit.

Greenwood: Greenlake/Greenwood Market Greenwood/LoyalHeights Market

Known as the area around 85th Street and Greenwood Avenue, it is booming with coffeehouses, antique stores, bars, and eateries. One of my favorite restaurants to visit is Flintcreek Cattle and is located on corner of 85th and Greenwood Ave N. Once a year, Greenwood hosts the Greenwood Car Show that happens every June and the Seafair Parade in August. The Seafair parade is complete with Seafair Pirates and a synchronized motorcycle routine by the local police force. The monthly art walks showcase not only local artist, but give you a chance to tour all of the interesting businesses along Greenwood Ave. If you love classic cars, the Car Show is definitely the event for you and right after the Car Show you can head over to any number of breweries right around the corner. If you're hungry, their is also a rotating schedule of daily food trucks - making it the perfect spot for a quick bite and a beer! And if you have a sweet tooth, don't forget the homemade truffles at Chocolati Cafe.

Capitol Hill: Capitol Hill Market North Capitol Hill Market

Arguably one of Seattle's most popular nightlife and entertainment neighborhoods. Much like Queen Anne, Capitol Hill is situated on a steep hill that is east of the city's downtown central business district. One thing I loved and miss about living on Capitol Hill is that I was always super close to the freeway which reduced my commute times drastically and you're just minutes from downtown Seattle. Capitol Hill's biggest street is Broadway and it's absolutely packed with shops, apartment buildings, restaurants, bars, specialty stores, and much much more. If you are ever on Broadway and craving something delicious, Seattle's famous burger chain Dick's is located right in the middle of E Olive Way and E Denny Way. If you're a foodie, Dick's is definitely worth a try. Not too far from Dick's is Seattle's Startbucks Reserve Roastery and is located on 1124 Pike St. This location has ample room to relax, have a delicious snack, and enjoy the rarest, most extraordinary coffee that Starbucks has to offer. If you're on Boren Ave, Little Neon Taco serves Mexican fare with modern flair; don't miss the mole chicken wings or the carnitas torta.

In addition to amazing food and coffee, I also recommend a visit to Blade & Timber (206 Broadway E) and try your hand in some axe-throwing, Garage Billiars & Bowl (1130 Broadway) for some food and entertainment, Rock Box (1603 Nagle Pl) for some karaoke in one of the twelve private rooms with full service or hang out in the lively bar and broadcast your talents to a larger crowd, or No Parking on Pike (1102 E Pike St) which is a hodgepodge of quirky collectibles and vintage home decor.

West Seattle: WestSeattle Market West Seattle Fauntleroy Market

Alki Beach is arguably one of West Seattle's biggest attractions! You can rent bikes, inline skates, stand-up paddleboards, and kayaks from Alki Kayak Tours(1660 Harbor Ave SW) or you can relax with a Sounder Stout at West Seattle Brewing Co. (4415 Fauntleroy Way SW). Not a fan of beer, or perhaps you're in the mood for some cider? Locust Cider (2820 Alike Ave) has exactly what you're looking for; you can check out their cold gluten-free flight while enjoying an incredible view of Puget Sound. For a waterside stroll, visit Lincoln Park and keep an eye out for seals while enjoying views of the Olympic Mountain Range. On Sundays, sample regional produce at the year-round West Seattle Farmer Market (SW Alaska St & California Ave SW).

The heart of West Seattle's retail district is the Junction. It is a lively three-block stretch that has something for everyone, whether you're looking for a vintage vinyl, or locally made. Boutiques range from a gift shop Capers (4525 California Ave SW) to Click! Design that Fits (4540 California Ave SW), which houses an array of Pacific Northwest-centric offerings. Easy Street Records (4559 California Ave SW) is a draw for record afficionados and casual browsers alike, and also has a popular cafe.

If you're in the mood for something delicious, West Seattle is bountiful! Grab a morning latte beachside at Ampersand Cafe on Alki (2536 Alki Ave SW) and a pastry from the beloved Bakery Nouveau (4737 California Ave SW), where twice-baked croissants are just the tip of the iceberg. In the mood for Hawaiian-fusion? Don't miss Ma'ono (4437 California Ave SW), where fried chicken is absolutely legendary, and waterfront mainstay Marination Ma Kai (1660 Harbor Ave SW) whose Spam sliders go great with kimchi fried rice. Or perhaps you're in the mood for some Italian. If so, you must check out Raccolto (4147 California Ave SW) or beautiful new Il Nido (2717 61st Ave SW). If you're in the mood for some house made ice cream, the Husky Deli is the spot with over a dozen flavors like French orange or rum truffle and to top it all off, a Prohibition-inspired nightcap, knock three times at The Alley (4509 California Ave SW)!

Last but not least! This is by no means a complete list of all the wonderful neighborhoods Seattle has to offer. These are just the neighborhoods that I either grew up in or frequently visited.
